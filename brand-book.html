01

Purpose

We help people move between homes in Central Massachusetts with the care that decision deserves.

Home is personal. Our job is to make one of the largest decisions in a family's life feel understood, unhurried and well guided.

Positioning

For buyers and sellers in Worcester County who want a knowledgeable person beside them, The Oberdorfer Team is a residential real-estate team that leads with knowledge of place and personal guidance.

Portals show everything and explain nothing. High-volume agencies move quickly and move on. We explain the market, the town and the house before we ask for anything, and we stay with people through the parts nobody photographs.

The promise

You will always know where things stand, what we think, and why.

Every page, message and listing should be checked against this line. If a reader would finish it still guessing, it is not done.

Three commitments

Knowledge of place

Towns, streets, school calendars, commuter times, how a 1920s house behaves in February. Specific, local and written down.

Personal guidance

Two team leads who answer their own phones, explain trade-offs plainly and give an opinion when asked.

Considered presentation

Homes are photographed, described and priced as if they were being published, because they are.

02

Audience

We write for one person at a time: someone deciding where to live next, often months before they say so.

Who reads us

Four readers, weighted equally. The site should never make one of them feel like an afterthought.

Buyers
First-time buyers comparing towns, families moving up, people relocating into Worcester County. Asking: Which town fits us, what does our budget buy, and what happens next? Afraid of: overpaying or missing something about the house.
Sellers
Long-time owners, people downsizing, families handling an estate. Asking: What is it worth, and how will it be presented? Afraid of: leaving money on the table and months of disruption.
Readers not yet searching
People reading town pages and Field Notes a season or a year ahead. Asking: What is it actually like to live there? Owed: real writing, with no contact form standing in the way.
People we work alongside
REWAP Brokerage, lenders, attorneys, inspectors and other agents. Asking: Are these people organized and easy to work with? Owed: clean listings, correct attribution, prompt answers.

Four rules

Say the specific thing
Years, materials, minutes to the Pike, the name of the street. A detail a competitor could not copy is worth three adjectives.
Explain before asking
Every call to action follows something useful. We earn the form.
Write to one person
"You" and "we." Short sentences. No industry shorthand without a plain explanation beside it.
Keep it level
No exclamation points in listings, no urgency we did not create honestly, no superlatives we cannot show.

Words we leave out

Some are tired. Some describe people instead of homes, which fair-housing practice asks us to avoid.

Tired

  • stunning
  • dream home
  • nestled
  • won't last
  • turnkey
  • must-see
  • luxury (unless it is literally true)

Describe the home, not the buyer

  • perfect for families
  • ideal for young professionals
  • safe neighborhood
  • exclusive community
  • great for empty nesters

Write instead: "four bedrooms on one floor," "a fenced yard," "a seven-minute walk to the park," "single-level living."

Why a wordmark

Real-estate marks lean on roofs, keys and pins because the category needs a shortcut. This team does not: its name is a family name, and the people are the point. Wide-spaced capitals give the name presence at small sizes and read like the lettering on a measured drawing or a magazine masthead.

The spacing is the identity. Keep it at 0.38em in every application, and never letterspace the brokerage line to match it.

Clear space and size

The Oberdorfer Team

Clear space
One cap height (x) on every side. Nothing enters it, including photographs.
Minimum size
130px wide on screen, 1.3 in. in print. Below that, use the compact form.
Compact form
OBERDORFER alone, same spacing, for tight headers and social bios.

Where a square is required

O

A single serif O on shutter green, only for favicons and social avatars where the full name cannot fit. It never appears beside the wordmark or replaces it anywhere else.

Rules

Numbers
Prices, square footage, dates and stats always use Archivo with tabular figures, so columns of listings line up.
Italics
Inria Serif Italic carries a property's architectural character line and the occasional emphasis. Never for whole paragraphs.
Capitals
Reserved for the wordmark and small meta labels. Headlines stay in sentence case.
Small screens
Body never drops below 17px. Display sizes scale with the viewport and stop at 96px, except the cover line.

07

Photography

Real houses and real places, in the light they actually get. We crop like architects and caption like editors.

Fig. 07 — Gambrel house above a stone wall. Sample photograph, LXS Photography / Unsplash.

Direction

Light is the subject
Low autumn sun, flat winter light, the hour when a room is at its best. Shoot when the house looks like itself, not when the sky is bluest.
Crop like an architect
Corners, stair runs, window reveals, the line where clapboard meets stone. One tight detail beside one wide view.
Places, not props
No keys in hands, no sold signs, no staged handshakes, no generic stock models.
People only when real
Clients who have agreed to it, the team at work, neighbors in their own places.
Always captioned
Figure number, what it shows, where, and who made it. A caption turns a picture into information.

Do and don't

Do

  • Shoot exteriors in the season the house will sell in.
  • Include one detail that shows how it was built.
  • Keep verticals straight; correct lens distortion.
  • Write alt text that describes the house, not the pitch.

Don't

  • Replace skies or add fake twilight glow.
  • Use wide-angle stretch to make rooms look larger.
  • Use people holding keys or pointing at houses.
  • Publish a photo of a real home without permission.

08

Layout

Pages are built as spreads: a photograph and a page of text that answer each other. One strong move per screen.

The grid

Columns
Twelve, with 16–32px gutters. Side margins match the cover's inset, about 3.5% of the viewport.
Asymmetry
Prefer 7 + 5 and 8 + 4 splits. A centered layout is reserved for the colophon and forms.
Stock regions
Color fills whole bands edge to edge. It is never a rounded card floating on the page.
Corners and rules
Square corners everywhere. Hairline rules separate; a 2px slate rule starts a list or a table.
Rhythm
Follow a dense passage with a quiet one: a full photograph, a single statement, white space.

Templates

Proportional schematics. Hatching marks photography.

Motion

Motion behaves like turning a page: a clean wipe, a settle, then stillness. Nothing bounces and nothing floats.

The masked reveal

Opening photographs wipe in from the left over 900ms while the image settles from 105% to 100%. It runs once, only for photographs still below the fold. The page is complete without it.

Fig. 14 — Bay window, afternoon light. Sample photograph, Stuart Jenkins / Unsplash.

Timing

TokenValueUse
--dur-quick160msHover, press, save
--dur-base320msFolio swap, sheets, menus
--dur-reveal900msOpening photographs
--ease-settlecubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1)Everything

Where motion lives

Running folio
The chapter or section name slides up into the folio as you read. On the site, the same bar carries the current town or listing.
Gallery
Photographs slide edge to edge at the settle curve; swiping follows the finger with no rubber-banding.
Hover
Underlines draw in, buttons darken. Images never zoom on hover.
Reduced motion
If the reader asks for less motion, every reveal, slide and swap becomes an instant change.

11

Access

Everyone looking for a home should be able to use the site. WCAG 2.1 AA is the floor, not the goal.

Commitments

Contrast
Only the tested pairs in chapter 06. Text never sits directly on a photograph without a stock panel or a measured scrim.
Keyboard
Every control reachable in order, with a visible brick focus ring (2px, offset 3px; clay on green).
Touch
Targets at least 44 × 44px. Sticky actions stay clear of the phone's home indicator.
Images
Alt text describes the house or place plainly. Decorative numerals and textures are hidden from screen readers.
Forms
Visible labels, never placeholder-only. Errors say what went wrong and how to fix it, next to the field.
Maps
Every map has a list equivalent and a text address.
Motion
Respects the reader's reduced-motion setting everywhere.

Fair housing

We describe homes and places, never the people who should live in them. Town pages report facts (distances, housing types, public amenities) and avoid characterizing who lives somewhere. Every page carries the Equal Housing Opportunity statement in the footer.

Massachusetts and federal fair-housing rules apply to all advertising. Have REWAP's principal broker confirm the final footer language and any required disclosures before launch.
