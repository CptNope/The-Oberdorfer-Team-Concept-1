## The concept

**The Quarterly.** The brand is presented like an independent architecture magazine printed on New England stock. Colored stock (shutter green, oak) owns covers and chapter openers; reading pages are cool plaster; brick is the one spot ink. It deliberately refuses two defaults: the brokerage template (aerial hero, centered search bar, listing grid) and the soft cream-and-serif look.

The cover is a shutter-green panel with the line "Home is personal." in large serif, a tall cropped photograph of clapboard and shutters, an "Open the book" action, and a one-row index of the twelve chapters. The second screen is a contents spread: a stair-hall photograph on the left, the numbered contents on the right.

**Signature interaction:** a running folio at the top of the page shows the current chapter as you read, and each chapter's opening photograph is revealed by a clean masked wipe.

## 01 Purpose

We help people move between homes in Central Massachusetts with the care that decision deserves.

**Purpose.** Home is personal. Our job is to make one of the largest decisions in a family's life feel understood, unhurried and well guided.

**Positioning.** For buyers and sellers in Worcester County who want a knowledgeable person beside them, The Oberdorfer Team is a residential real-estate team that leads with knowledge of place and personal guidance. Portals show everything and explain nothing. High-volume agencies move quickly and move on. We explain the market, the town and the house before we ask for anything, and we stay with people through the parts nobody photographs.

**The promise.** You will always know where things stand, what we think, and why.

**Three commitments**

| Commitment | What it means |
|---|---|
| Knowledge of place | Towns, streets, school calendars, commuter times, how a 1920s house behaves in February. Specific, local and written down. |
| Personal guidance | Two team leads who answer their own phones, explain trade-offs plainly and give an opinion when asked. |
| Considered presentation | Homes are photographed, described and priced as if they were being published, because they are. |

**How people should feel**

| Stage | They should feel | Because we |
|---|---|---|
| Before contact | Curious, under no pressure | Publish useful town and market writing with no gate in front of it. |
| First conversation | Oriented and heard | Ask about the life before the house, then explain the process in order. |
| Searching or listing | Calm, informed | Send fewer, better updates with our reasoning attached. |
| Offer and negotiation | Steady, protected | Lay out options and risks in writing, then recommend one. |
| After the move | Looked after | Stay useful: contractor names, tax dates, a note in a year. |

**What the brand is and is not**

| It is | It is not |
|---|---|
| A local guide with an opinion | A listings portal |
| Warm and exact | Salesy or breathless |
| Crafted, like a good renovation | Luxury for its own sake |
| Calm, even when the market isn't | Urgent, flashy, countdown-driven |
| Two named people and their team | A faceless agency template |

## 02 Audience

We write for one person at a time: someone deciding where to live next, often months before they say so. Four readers, weighted equally.

| Reader | Who | Asking | Owed / afraid of |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buyers | First-time buyers comparing towns, families moving up, people relocating into Worcester County | Which town fits us, what does our budget buy, what happens next? | Afraid of overpaying or missing something about the house |
| Sellers | Long-time owners, people downsizing, families handling an estate | What is it worth, and how will it be presented? | Afraid of leaving money on the table and months of disruption |
| Readers not yet searching | People reading town pages and Field Notes a season or a year ahead | What is it actually like to live there? | Owed real writing, with no contact form in the way |
| People we work alongside | REWAP Brokerage, lenders, attorneys, inspectors, other agents | Are these people organized and easy to work with? | Owed clean listings, correct attribution, prompt answers |

**Where each reader lands**

| Reader | Likely first page | What should come next |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer | A listing, or a town page from search | The town's housing character, then the buying guide |
| Seller | "What is my home worth?" | How we present and price, then a conversation |
| Not yet searching | A Field Notes article | Related towns, then a saved search when they are ready |
| Partner | Team page or a listing | Agent bios with brokerage attribution and direct contact |

## 03 Voice

We sound like a well-informed neighbor: specific, level and kind. We never shout, and we never sell past the facts.

**Personality**

| We are | Without becoming |
|---|---|
| Knowledgeable | Lecturing |
| Warm | Familiar or cute |
| Precise | Cold or legalistic |
| Calm | Passive |
| Plainspoken | Casual about serious things |

**Four rules**

1. **Say the specific thing.** Years, materials, minutes to the Pike, the name of the street. A detail a competitor could not copy is worth three adjectives.
2. **Explain before asking.** Every call to action follows something useful. We earn the form.
3. **Write to one person.** "You" and "we." Short sentences. No industry shorthand without a plain explanation beside it.
4. **Keep it level.** No exclamation points in listings, no urgency we did not create honestly, no superlatives we cannot show.

**Messaging hierarchy** (each level supports the one above)

1. Brand line — *Home is personal.*
2. Positioning line — *Real estate guidance for Central Massachusetts.*
3. Proof themes — Knowledge of place · Considered presentation · Clear communication
4. Invitations — Talk with us · Request a showing · Ask what your home is worth · Save this search
5. Attribution — The Oberdorfer Team at REWAP Brokerage LLC

**Rewrites** (sample copy; the listing is illustrative)

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Stunning turnkey colonial nestled in a sought-after neighborhood. Won't last! | A 1926 center-hall colonial on a quiet side street, with original oak floors, a kitchen renovated in 2019 and a level backyard. About twelve minutes to Union Station. |
| Get your FREE instant home value now! | Ask what your home is worth. We will send a written range with the sales we used, within [turnaround to confirm]. |

**Words we leave out**

- Tired: *stunning, dream home, nestled, won't last, turnkey, must-see, luxury* (unless literally true).
- Describe the home, not the buyer (fair-housing practice): *perfect for families, ideal for young professionals, safe neighborhood, exclusive community, great for empty nesters.*
- Write instead: "four bedrooms on one floor," "a fenced yard," "a seven-minute walk to the park," "single-level living."

## 04 Identity

A wordmark, set the way an architect letters a title block: spaced capitals, no symbol, nothing to explain.

- **Primary wordmark:** THE OBERDORFER TEAM in Inria Serif Regular, all capitals, tracked 0.38em. Shutter green on plaster, or plaster reversed on green.
- **Affiliation lockups:** inline ("The Oberdorfer Team · at REWAP Brokerage LLC") and stacked (THE OBERDORFER / TEAM / at REWAP Brokerage LLC). The brokerage line is Archivo at width 82.
- **Why a wordmark:** real-estate marks lean on roofs, keys and pins because the category needs a shortcut. This team does not: its name is a family name, and the people are the point. The spacing is the identity; keep it at 0.38em everywhere and never letterspace the brokerage line to match it.
- **Clear space:** one cap height on every side. Nothing enters it, including photographs.
- **Minimum size:** 130px wide on screen, 1.3 in. in print. Below that, use the compact form, **OBERDORFER** alone with the same spacing.
- **Square avatar:** a single serif O on shutter green, only for favicons and social avatars. Never beside or instead of the wordmark elsewhere.
- **Misuse:** don't reset the name in another face or with default spacing; don't set the brokerage line larger than the team name; don't use unlisted color pairs (e.g. green on brick); don't add a roof, key, pin or house symbol.

## 05 Typography

One serif to read and to headline; one grotesk that narrows for labels and widens for prices. Both are open source (SIL OFL) on Google Fonts.

- **Inria Serif** — display and reading. Light, Regular and Bold with italics. Bold for headlines, Regular for reading at 17–21px, Light only for the giant chapter numerals.
- **Archivo** — interface and data, with a variable width axis (62–125). Width 82 for labels and captions, 100 for buttons and navigation, 116 for prices.

**Scale**

| Role | Spec |
|---|---|
| Cover line | Inria Serif Bold, ~232px at a 2528px frame (scales with the viewport), line 0.86 |
| Display | Inria Serif Bold · 56–96px · line 0.94 · −0.012em |
| Heading 1 | Inria Serif Bold · 36–60px · line 1.02 |
| Heading 2 | Inria Serif Bold · 28–40px · line 1.1 |
| Heading 3 | Archivo Semibold · width 92 · 22px |
| Body | Inria Serif Regular · 17–19px · line 1.6 · 64ch max |
| Meta | Archivo · width 82 · 13px · capitals · +0.06em |
| Caption | Archivo · width 82 · 13px · line 1.4 |
| Price | Archivo Medium · width 116 · 24–34px · tabular figures |
| Property data | Archivo · width 100 · 15px · tabular figures |

**Rules**

- Prices, square footage, dates and stats always use Archivo with tabular figures, so listing columns line up.
- Inria Serif Italic carries a property's architectural character line and the occasional emphasis, never whole paragraphs.
- Capitals are reserved for the wordmark and small meta labels; headlines stay in sentence case.
- Body never drops below 17px. Display sizes scale with the viewport and stop at 96px, except the cover line.

## 07 Photography

Real houses and real places, in the light they actually get. We crop like architects and caption like editors.

**Direction**

- **Light is the subject.** Low autumn sun, flat winter light, the hour when a room is at its best. Shoot when the house looks like itself, not when the sky is bluest.
- **Crop like an architect.** Corners, stair runs, window reveals, the line where clapboard meets stone. One tight detail beside one wide view.
- **Places, not props.** No keys in hands, no sold signs, no staged handshakes, no generic stock models.
- **People only when real.** Clients who have agreed to it, the team at work, neighbors in their own places.
- **Always captioned.** Figure number, what it shows, where, and who made it.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Shoot exteriors in the season the house will sell in | Replace skies or add fake twilight glow |
| Include one detail that shows how it was built | Use wide-angle stretch to make rooms look larger |
| Keep verticals straight; correct lens distortion | Use people holding keys or pointing at houses |
| Write alt text that describes the house, not the pitch | Publish a photo of a real home without permission |

**Replace before launch.** Every photograph in the book is a free Unsplash sample (see [Credits](#credits)). The shot list for a real shoot: a Worcester County house the team has sold (cover, corner view, low sun); an interior detail from a real listing; Brandon and Kait's own neighborhood; a winter detail from a Central Mass. town center; painted lettering on a Worcester mill building; material close-ups from listings; town-page openers for the launch towns; listing photography used with seller permission.

## 08 Layout

Pages are built as spreads: a photograph and a page of text that answer each other. One strong move per screen.

- **Columns:** twelve, with 16–32px gutters. Side margins match the cover's inset, about 3.5% of the viewport.
- **Asymmetry:** prefer 7 + 5 and 8 + 4 splits. Centered layouts are reserved for the colophon and forms.
- **Stock regions:** color fills whole bands edge to edge, never a rounded card floating on the page.
- **Corners and rules:** square corners everywhere. Hairlines separate; a 2px slate rule starts a list or table.
- **Rhythm:** follow a dense passage with a quiet one: a full photograph, a single statement, white space.

**Templates:** homepage (green cover, discovery, featured homes, towns, valuation panel on oak); property detail (gallery, facts, story, map, attribution, showing request in reach); town page (wide opener, narrative, housing character, homes, market notes).

**The homepage, in ten movements** (replaces the usual hero, three icons and a grid)

1. **Home is personal.** The cover line on green, one photograph, the team and brokerage named.
2. **Start looking.** Search by town, price and type, with a map one tap away.
3. **Why the Oberdorfers.** Two people, how they work, what clients can expect in writing.
4. **Homes we're showing.** Three listings presented editorially, one large and two small.
5. **Towns we know.** Openers for the launch towns, each linking to a full town page.
6. **Buying, in order.** The six-step journey, with what we do at each step.
7. **Selling, with care.** Presentation and pricing, then the valuation panel on oak.
8. **Our story.** Brandon and Kait, the team and how it grows.
9. **Field Notes.** Market commentary and local writing, most recent first.
10. **Talk with us.** A short form, direct contacts, brokerage attribution.

## 09 Interface

Listings read like features in an architecture magazine: the photograph, the address, the price, and what makes the house itself.

**Controls.** Square, quiet, always labeled with the action. One brick button per view (e.g. "Request a showing"); secondary actions are outlined or written as links; on green, the primary button is plaster. Disabled buttons drop to recessed plaster and say what is happening ("Sending…"). Form fields have visible labels, a paper fill and square borders; errors appear next to the field and say how to fix it.

**Property presentation** (all sample listings)

- **Featured property:** a large photograph with an "Open house" tag and a Save toggle; address in serif, town, price in wide Archivo, facts row (beds · baths · sq ft · acres), an italic architectural-character line ("Circa 1780 center-chimney colonial: wide pine floors, six-over-six windows, a 2019 kitchen in the old ell"), a showing request, and the attribution line.
- **Compact card:** same order every time — photo, address, town, price, year, facts, character line.
- **Search results:** a list that scans like a table of contents, prices aligned. IDX listings from other brokerages carry "Listing courtesy of [Listing Brokerage] · MLS PIN".
- **Property detail:** gallery (main image plus two, "All 32 photos"), fact table (bedrooms, baths, living area, lot, built, style, heat, taxes), a written house story by the team (never generated from listing fields), a brand-styled map with a price marker instead of red pins, and a sticky "Request a showing" panel.
- **Save state:** a bookmark toggle with `aria-pressed`, "Save" / "Saved".

**On a phone:** photography first, sticky Save and Request-a-showing actions under the thumb, filters in a bottom sheet (towns, price range, bedrooms, "Show 38 homes"), and a map that trades places with the list.

**Buying, in order**

| Step | Buyer decides | We |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Discover | Which towns, what kind of house, what budget feels right | First conversation, town notes, a lender introduction |
| 2 Understand | What the market is doing in the chosen towns | Recent sales explained, a saved search tuned to you |
| 3 Tour | Seeing homes, and seeing past the staging | Notes on each house, questions for the seller |
| 4 Offer | Price, terms and contingencies | Comparable sales, options in writing, a recommendation |
| 5 Close | Inspection, appraisal, financing, paperwork | One timeline, every deadline, the attorney looped in |
| 6 Move | Keys, utilities and the first season | Contractor names, tax dates, a check-in a year later |

**Selling, with care:** presentation (professional photography, a written story of the house, room-by-room preparation advice); strategy (timing, buyer profile, launch plan agreed in writing); local market (what sold nearby, how long it took and why); pricing (a range with the reasoning shown, and a plan if the market answers differently); marketing (listing page, Field Notes features, open houses, targeted local promotion); negotiation (offers side by side, net proceeds, a clear recommendation); communication (a weekly written update, whether or not anything changed).

**Valuation panel** (on oak): "What is your home worth?" — a written range with the sales used and what would move it, within [turnaround to confirm]; no obligation and no automated estimate. Shown once on the homepage, on every selling page and in town-page footers. Never a pop-up.

**Town pages** — worth reading even when nobody is shopping. Each has a narrative introduction (300–600 words in the team's voice) plus housing character, an architecture photo essay, live listings with attribution, a styled map, parks and outdoors, eating and errands, monthly market notes from MLS data, and related Field Notes. Written and fact-checked by the team.

**Field Notes** — the recommended name for the editorial program (alternatives: Home Notes, Around Here, The Journal). Four recurring kinds: market commentary, town guides, house stories, and practical guides for buying, selling and renovating.

## 10 Motion

Motion behaves like turning a page: a clean wipe, a settle, then stillness. Nothing bounces and nothing floats.

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--dur-quick` | 160ms | Hover, press, save |
| `--dur-base` | 320ms | Folio swap, sheets, menus |
| `--dur-reveal` | 900ms | Opening photographs |
| `--ease-settle` | `cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1)` | Everything |

- **Masked reveal:** opening photographs wipe in from the left over 900ms while the image settles from 105% to 100%. It runs once, only for photographs still below the fold; the page is complete without it.
- **Running folio:** the chapter (or, on the site, the current town or listing) slides into the top bar as you read.
- **Gallery:** photographs slide edge to edge at the settle curve.
- **Hover:** underlines draw in, buttons darken. Images never zoom on hover.
- **Reduced motion:** every reveal, slide and swap becomes an instant change.

## 11 Access

Everyone looking for a home should be able to use the site. WCAG 2.1 AA is the floor, not the goal.

- **Contrast:** only the tested pairs in 06 Color. Text never sits directly on a photograph without a stock panel or a measured scrim.
- **Keyboard:** every control reachable in order, with a visible focus ring (brick 2px, offset 3px; clay on green; deep ink on oak).
- **Touch:** targets at least 44 × 44px; sticky actions clear of the phone's home indicator.
- **Images:** alt text describes the house or place plainly; decorative numerals and textures are hidden from screen readers.
- **Forms:** visible labels, never placeholder-only; errors say what went wrong and how to fix it, next to the field.
- **Maps:** every map has a list equivalent and a text address.
- **Motion:** respects the reader's reduced-motion setting everywhere.
- **Fair housing:** describe homes and places, never who should live in them. Every page carries the Equal Housing Opportunity statement. REWAP's principal broker should confirm final footer language and required disclosures before launch.

## 12 Affiliation

The Oberdorfer Team is the name people know. REWAP Brokerage LLC is the brokerage behind every transaction. Both are always visible, and never confused.

**The rule.** The team owns its brand and its writing. The brokerage remains the authority on every transaction. The brokerage line is set in Archivo width 82 at 60% of the wordmark size, never below 12px on screen or 7pt in print. REWAP appears typographically only.

**Where it appears** (bracketed items supplied by REWAP and confirmed by its principal broker)

- **Navigation:** the lockup sits in the header on every page; on phones the brokerage line moves under the name.
- **Agent bio:** "Brandon Oberdorfer — Team lead · Massachusetts real estate broker [license #] · The Oberdorfer Team at REWAP Brokerage LLC · [phone] · [email]". Every bio names the brokerage. Kait's role and license details to be confirmed.
- **Listing attribution:** our listings — "Listed by The Oberdorfer Team at REWAP Brokerage LLC · MLS PIN #[number]"; other brokerages' IDX listings — "Listing courtesy of [Listing Brokerage] · Data © MLS PIN, deemed reliable but not guaranteed".
- **Contact forms:** "Your message goes to The Oberdorfer Team at REWAP Brokerage LLC. We use it only to reply to you. [Privacy policy]"
- **Footer and legal:** "The Oberdorfer Team is a team of real estate professionals affiliated with REWAP Brokerage LLC, Worcester, Massachusetts. Brokerage services are provided by REWAP Brokerage LLC. [Brokerage address] · [Phone] · [License information]. Equal Housing Opportunity. Listing information is deemed reliable but not guaranteed. [IDX disclaimer] · [Privacy policy] · [Accessibility statement]"

**One platform, two brands.** The team site and a future REWAP site can share data without duplicating it:

```
Brokerage (REWAP Brokerage LLC, authority)
  └── Team (The Oberdorfer Team, brand) ── publishes ──> Article / Town page (owned by the team)
        └── Agent (Brandon, Kait, future agents)
IDX adapter (vendor-neutral, MLS PIN feed) ── supplies ──> Property (one record per MLS listing)
Lead ── references ──> Property, Team, Agent, Brokerage ── forwards to CRM when connected
```

The IDX adapter normalizes any vendor's feed into one property shape, so the front end, saved searches and lead routing never depend on a single vendor.

## The website concept

The website is the Quarterly's next issue. It uses the brand book's system unchanged (DESIGN.md): every page opens on a colored stock band with a photograph and continues on plaster reading pages, the homepage runs as the book's ten movements, and the brokerage line sits in every header, listing, form and footer. It refuses the IDX template: an aerial hero with a centered search box, a twelve-card listing grid, chat bubbles and countdown banners.

**What works in the demo**

- **Running folio.** A thin bar under each page's opener names where you are and updates as you scroll: the homepage movement, the town section, or the listing's address and price.
- **Saved homes.** The bookmark toggle on any card, row or listing saves it in your browser. The header shows the count, saved homes print in brick on the search map, and "Saved only" filters the list. Nothing leaves the browser.
- **Search.** Town, price, bedrooms and type filters, four sort orders, an empty state, and filters carried in the URL (`homes.html?town=Holden`). Hovering a result lights its map marker. On a phone the map and list trade places with one toggle.
- **Listing gallery.** "All 6 photos" opens a full-screen gallery that slides edge to edge, with arrow keys and swipe. Interior photographs are labeled as samples that are not of the house.
- **Forms built for Follow Up Boss.** Every form (showing request, valuation, contact, open-house sign-in, save this search, keep me posted) asks for first and last name, email and phone (one of the two is required) and an optional call/text consent. They validate inline, send nothing, and show the exact Follow Up Boss event they would send: the request headers and the JSON body, with the property or search, page and campaign filled in (try `?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=paid_social&utm_campaign=fall-open-houses`).
- **Saved homes and searches.** Saved homes and saved searches collect on the Saved homes page; "Keep me posted" turns them into a Registration event plus one Saved Property event per home.
- **Phone patterns.** Sticky Save and Request-a-showing actions on listings, a full-width menu sheet, single-column cards and stacked forms.

**The pages**

| Page | Structure |
|---|---|
| Home | 1 Cover on green: "Home is personal.", the team and brokerage named, one October photograph. 2 Start looking: town, price, bedrooms, type. 3 Why the Oberdorfers: three commitments and the written promise. 4 Homes we're showing: one featured listing and two compact cards. 5 Towns we know: Shrewsbury, then Holden, Worcester and Grafton as filtered searches. 6 Buying, in order. 7 Selling, with care, then the valuation panel on oak. 8 Our story. 9 Field Notes. 10 Talk with us. |
| Homes for sale | Green type band, a filter bar, results as a table of contents (thumbnail, address, town, facts, aligned price, Save), IDX attribution on other brokerages' listings, and a sticky illustrative map with price markers. |
| Listing | Gallery first (main photograph plus two), sample tag, address, town line, price, Save, open house, the eight-cell fact table, the team-written story, an illustrative street map with a price marker, attribution, a sticky showing-request panel, and three nearby homes. |
| Shrewsbury | Full opener (lake photograph with an oak card), the town in three paragraphs, an at-a-glance slot grid, homes here now, an architecture photo essay, a map with a list equivalent, parks and outdoors, market notes held as `[MLS PIN]` placeholders, related Field Notes, and the valuation panel. |
| Buying | Six steps, each with what you decide and what the team does, including the Massachusetts relationship disclosure, written buyer agreements, lead paint notification, Offer to Purchase, Purchase and Sale, mortgage commitment and attorney closings; a glossary; costs to plan for. |

**Sample data.** Eleven sample listings across nine towns (Auburn, Boylston, Grafton, Holden, Northborough, Princeton, Shrewsbury, Westborough, Worcester) live in `tools/site_data.py`. Eight are presented as the team's own listings and three as other brokerages' IDX listings with "Listing courtesy of [Listing Brokerage]". Addresses, prices, taxes and stories are invented for the demo and labeled "Sample" everywhere they appear.

**Kept honest.** Market figures, bios, license numbers, phone numbers, the brokerage address and reply times stay bracketed placeholders. The service commitments in the copy (plans in writing, weekly written updates) are the brand book's proposals and are marked for the team to confirm. Town facts on the Shrewsbury page are kept to stable geography (the lake, Route 9, Route 20, I-290, the commuter rail stations in Grafton and Westborough) and the page is labeled sample copy to be written and fact-checked by the team.

## Lead handoff to Follow Up Boss

The site is built to hand every lead to Follow Up Boss through its Events API. The full spec, with an example request, is on the [handoff page](https://cptnope.github.io/The-Oberdorfer-Team-Concept-1/follow-up-boss.html).

**Path.** The browser posts the form to the team's own server (a WordPress REST endpoint), never to Follow Up Boss directly. The server sends `POST https://api.followupboss.com/v1/events` with HTTP Basic auth (API key as the username, blank password) and the `X-System` / `X-System-Key` headers issued when the site is registered as a system. Follow Up Boss matches people by email or phone, creates or updates the contact, and runs the lead flow for the source. It answers `201` (new person), `200` (existing person) or `204` (source archived).

| Site action | Event `type` | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| Request a showing (every listing) | Property Inquiry | `property` |
| Ask what it's worth (home, selling, town pages) | Seller Inquiry | `property` parsed from the address |
| Talk with us: selling | Seller Inquiry | message, tags |
| Talk with us: buying, both, just reading, joining the team | General Inquiry | message, tags |
| Open-house sign-in | Visited Open House | `property`, agent status, "also selling" |
| Keep me posted (saved homes) | Registration, then Saved Property per home | `property` per home |
| Save this search | Saved Property Search | `propertySearch` |
| Listing views and searches by a known visitor (live site) | Viewed Property, Property Search | `property`, `propertySearch` |

**Fields.** `person.firstName`, `lastName`, `emails[]`, `phones[]`, `tags`, and a `customBrokerage` custom field; `property.street`, `city`, `state`, `code`, `mlsNumber`, `price`, `forRent`, `url`, `type`, `bedrooms`, `bathrooms`, `area`, `lot`; `propertySearch.city`, `state`, `type`, `maxPrice`, `minBedrooms`; `campaign.*` from UTM parameters (only when `utm_source` is present, since `campaign.source` is required); `message`, `description` (form, page, timing, agent status, and the consent line with its timestamp and exact wording), `pageUrl`, `pageTitle`, `pageReferrer`, `occurredAt`. `source` is the site's domain without "www" (to be chosen) and `system` is `OberdorferTeamSite`.

**To set up in Follow Up Boss before launch:** register the site as a system, create the API key (server-side only), name the source and its lead flow, write action plans for Property Inquiry, Seller Inquiry and Visited Open House, create the Brokerage custom field, confirm the consent wording with REWAP's principal broker, finish A2P 10DLC registration before relying on texts, give any old lead sources their own names, and test end to end.

Sources: Follow Up Boss developer documentation, [Events POST](https://docs.followupboss.com/reference/events-post), [Lead provider integration guide](https://docs.followupboss.com/docs/lead-provider-integration-guide), [IDX integration](https://docs.followupboss.com/reference/idx-integration), [Identification](https://docs.followupboss.com/reference/identification), [Authentication](https://docs.followupboss.com/reference/authentication).

```sh
python3 tools/build.py
```

Run it locally (the pages use relative paths, so any static server works):

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8000

```
